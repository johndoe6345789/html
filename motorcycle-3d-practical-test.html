Practical Test: motorcycle

The UK motorcycle test in two modules, ridden on an automatic bike with your wheel as the handlebars. Every move off, turn and pull-up needs a lifesaver: a real look over your shoulder.

Module 1

Off-road on the manoeuvring area: slalom and figure of eight, slow ride, U-turn, cornering and controlled stop, emergency stop, and hazard avoidance through the 50 km/h speed trap.

Module 2

On-road through the village with the examiner following on a bike and talking to you by radio: an angle start, junctions, a roundabout, a normal stop and a pull-up.

Wheel and pedals

No wheel detected yet. Turn the wheel or press a pedal to wake it up.

The bars reach full lock (38° each way) at the end of your wheel’s travel, so they track your wheel as a scaled copy. For one-to-one bars, set the rotation in the Thrustmaster control panel to 76° before calibrating; 180–270° feels more natural on a wheel. At speed the same input leans the bike instead of turning the bars, so hold the wheel over to hold a lean. Map two buttons for the lifesavers: they’re marked on every exercise.

Examiner

Automatic bike: no clutch or gears, and it won’t move until you open the throttle. Your foot goes down by itself below walking pace, which counts against you during the slow ride and U-turn.
No wheel to hand? Arrow keys or WASD ride, Q and E indicate, Z and C are the left and right lifesavers, T hides the report, Esc pauses.
